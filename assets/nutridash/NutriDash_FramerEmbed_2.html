02 - Problem Statement

What's
Broken

Health-conscious individuals struggle to find convenient meal delivery options that align with their dietary goals due to a lack of ingredient transparency, personalized recommendations, and intuitive UX. This leads to frustration, uninformed choices, and real health risks.

01
No Ingredient Transparency
Most food delivery apps don't provide detailed ingredient breakdowns, allergen alerts, or verified sourcing. Users rely on vague labels like "healthy" or "low-calorie" which mean nothing without context.
02
No Personalized Recommendations
Apps don't adapt to individual dietary needs - vegan, keto, gluten-free, or allergy-specific. Users must manually filter through hundreds of options every single time they order.
03
Decision Fatigue
Users spend excessive time researching meals outside the app or avoid food delivery altogether due to uncertainty about what they're actually eating.
04
Health Risk for Allergy Sufferers
Consumers with allergies or dietary restrictions may unknowingly consume harmful ingredients due to missing or unclear allergen information - a serious safety issue.

This Problem Clearly Defines:

Who is Affected
Health-conscious individuals and users with dietary restrictions or allergies
What the Issue Is
Difficulty finding meals that align with health goals due to lack of information
Why It Matters
Leads to frustration, uninformed choices, and potential health risks
What Needs to Change
Better ingredient transparency, AI personalization, and UX improvements

04 - User Personas

Who We're
Designing For

Find meals that fit his diet instantly - without opening a nutrition calculator or checking ingredient lists manually.
Pain Point

Situation

Goal
Order with complete confidence that her child's allergen needs are met - no fear, no second-guessing, no external research needed.
Pain Point
Allergen warnings are vague or missing entirely. Often calls restaurants directly to verify - a frustrating, time-consuming workaround.

06 - Design Process

6-Day
Design Sprint

I followed a structured sprint methodology - moving from problem understanding to tested prototypes in 6 focused days.

Day 01
Understand
Defined and framed the problem statement. Identified who is affected, what the core issue is, why it matters, and what needs to change. Built the problem foundation.
Day 02
Research
Conducted a 30-person Google Form survey. Extracted key metrics across feature preferences, AI adoption rates, and dietary priorities. Validated every assumption with real data.
Day 03
Redefine
Mapped the Who/What/How/When framework. Documented the before and after impact clearly. Sharpened the problem to a precise, actionable design brief.
Day 04
Ideate
Ran both convergent and divergent brainstorming. Identified 3 high-impact convergent solutions and 2 bold divergent concepts including a virtual nutritionist chatbot.
Day 05
Prototype
Created hand-drawn sketches to validate flow before screens. Built high-fidelity mockups in Figma for 4 core user flows - Order, Nutrition, Swap, and Dashboard.
Day 06
Test
Conducted in-person usability testing with 6 real users. Collected structured feedback and documented 4 key iterations - each grounded in a specific user pain point.

10 - Usability Testing

Real Users,
Real Feedback

This is what makes NutriDash stand out - real in-person usability testing with 6 actual users. Not assumptions. Not surveys alone. Real people, real reactions, real problems uncovered.

📸
In-Person Testing Sessions
6 usability testing sessions were conducted in person across different settings - cafes, offices, and co-working spaces. Users were given specific tasks to complete on the Figma prototype while I observed and took notes without interrupting their flow. Add your testing photos here to show the real sessions.
⏱️
Preparation Time Issue
Restaurant owners noted that healthy meals take longer to prepare, risking delays during peak hours. Users needed realistic visibility into wait times before ordering - not a surprise after placing the order.
💰
Cost Barrier
Several users found healthy meal options too expensive to order regularly. Healthy eating felt aspirational but not accessible. Affordability was a real blocker preventing consistent usage of the app.
📅
Scheduling Needs
Users wanted to align meal delivery with their daily routines - post-gym sessions, lunch breaks, or pre-planned weekly schedules. The ability to plan ahead was missing entirely from the initial design.
⌚
Health App Integration
Strong interest in syncing with Apple Watch, Google Fit, and Fitbit to automatically track calorie intake and daily activity. Users wanted their food choices to flow seamlessly into their existing health ecosystem.
11 - Iterations

Feedback In,
Changes Out

Based on usability testing I made 4 concrete design improvements - each directly addressing a specific user pain point discovered during testing.

01
Prep Time Badge
Feedback: Healthy meals take longer to prepare, risking delays during peak hours.
Introduced a Prep Time Badge on each meal card (e.g., "15 min", "30 min") so users know what to expect before ordering
Added a "Quick and Healthy" filter for meals that take 20 minutes or less
Enabled restaurants to set prep-time limits during busy hours to manage order flow
02
Subscription Meal Plans
Feedback: Some users found healthy meals too expensive to order regularly.
Partnered with restaurants to create budget-friendly healthy combo meals
Added weekly subscription plans with discounted pricing for regular users
Designed a Meal Credit reward system - users earn points for consistent ordering which reduces future costs
03
Smart Delivery Scheduling
Feedback: Users wanted meal delivery aligned with their daily routines - post-gym, lunch break, or weekly planning.
Built a smart scheduling feature - delivery slot selection based on time of day, activity type, or calendar sync
Added ability to save routine schedules for faster future orders
Push reminders: "It's 1 PM! Would you like to reorder your usual healthy lunch?"
04
Gamification for Motivation
Feedback: Users needed motivation to stick to healthy eating habits long-term.
Added gamification - users earn badges for consistency (e.g., "Healthy Streak: 7 Days")
Daily wellness tips and healthy eating facts to educate and engage users
Health progress tracking integrated with Apple Health and Fitbit

13 - Reflection

What I
Learned

✦
Real research changes everything
Having actual survey data from 30 respondents changed every design decision. Numbers like 56.7% and 40% aren't estimates - they're real user voices. Every designer should start with research, not assumptions. The data validated the problem before I drew a single wireframe.
✦
Usability testing reveals what you miss
I didn't anticipate the prep time issue or the cost barrier until real users raised them in testing. Testing with just 6 people uncovered 4 significant problems I had completely overlooked during the design phase. This is why testing is non-negotiable.
✦
Iterations show design maturity
Taking feedback and turning it into specific design changes - prep time badge, subscription plans, smart scheduling, gamification - is what separates a finished product from a concept. Each iteration is a design decision backed by real evidence.
✦
What I'd do differently
I'd define success metrics upfront - task completion rate on the ingredient panel, allergen alert click-through rate, repeat order frequency. Measuring impact from day one makes the design stronger and the case study far more compelling to hiring managers.
✦
The 50% "maybe" group is the real opportunity
Half of survey respondents were unsure about AI meal recommendations - not against it. This isn't a rejection signal, it's an education gap. The app needs to show the value of AI through onboarding and subtle UI nudges before asking users to trust it fully.
