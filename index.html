The Premise

From complex, to clear.

Most enterprise software isn't badly designed. It's undesigned, built feature by feature until nobody remembers what it was for.

I started at SharePoint Designs in 2022, and the brief was almost always the same: here is a system that technically works, and here are the people quietly avoiding it. Fifty-plus intranet portals later, I've learned the fix is rarely a new feature. It's a clearer sentence, a shorter path, a screen that admits what it's actually for.

So I work in the unglamorous middle, between the stakeholder who wants everything on the homepage, the developer who has to build it, and the person on the third floor who just needs to find one policy document. The case studies below are three versions of that same problem.

03
How I Work

Four moves, in order.

Not a methodology I read about. Just the sequence that stops me designing the wrong thing beautifully.

01

Listen

Talk to the people who have to live in the thing. Stakeholders describe the system they wanted; users describe the one they got.

02

Frame

Write the problem in one sentence everyone can agree on. If it takes a paragraph, we don't understand it yet.

03

Design

Flows before screens, systems before pixels. Components that a developer can reuse without asking me what the spacing was.

04

Ship

Stay through the build. The handoff isn't a file. It's the fifty small answers that come after it.
